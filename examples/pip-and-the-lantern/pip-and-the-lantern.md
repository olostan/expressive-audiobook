# Pip and the Lantern

## Chapter 1: The Night Visitor

On the edge of Willow Wood lived a small fox named Pip. Every evening, when the sun slid behind the hills, Pip curled up in her cosy den and listened to the rain tapping on the leaves above.

One stormy night, there was a knock at the door. Tap, tap, tap. Pip sat up straight. Nobody ever knocked at her door.

"Who is there?" whispered Pip.

"It is only me," said a tiny voice. "I am Wren, and I am very wet."

Pip opened the old wooden door, and in hopped a little bird, shivering from beak to tail. "Come in, come in," said Pip. "Sit by the fire, and I will find you a warm blanket."

Wren sneezed, and Pip giggled. Soon the two of them were sipping hot acorn tea and watching the flames dance.

"I am lost," said Wren at last. "My family is across the river, and I cannot see the way in the dark."

Pip thought for a long moment. Then her eyes lit up. "Grandfather's lantern!" she cried.

## Chapter 2: Across the River

The lantern hung high on a dusty hook at the back of the den. Pip stood on her tiptoes, stretched as far as she could, and pulled it down with a clatter.

When she lit it, a soft golden glow filled the room. It was the warmest, kindest light Pip had ever seen.

Together they stepped out into the night. The rain had stopped, and the forest was quiet except for the soft crunch of leaves under Pip's paws and the far-off hoot of an owl.

At the river, the stepping stones glistened in the lantern light. "Hold on tight," said Pip, and Wren climbed onto her shoulder.

Splash! One foot slipped, but Pip caught herself and laughed. Step by careful step, they crossed the water.

On the far bank, a chorus of tiny voices cheered. "Wren! Wren is home!"

Wren's whole family fluttered around them, chirping with joy. "Thank you, Pip," said Wren. "You are the best friend a bird could have."

Pip walked home beneath the stars, the lantern swinging gently, and for the first time she did not feel alone at all.
